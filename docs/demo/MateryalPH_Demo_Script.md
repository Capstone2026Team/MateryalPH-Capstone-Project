# MateryalPH demonstration script

Prepared: 20 October 2026. Language: simple English. Audience: advisers, panelists, and first-time users.

This is a spoken, page-by-page walkthrough of the Buyer mobile app, Vendor portal, and Admin portal. Read the **Say** paragraphs aloud and follow the **Show** instructions on screen. The full tour can take approximately 40–55 minutes with interactions; the shorter route below targets 15 minutes.

The script follows the approved workflows and the current application routes and navigation inspected for this document. It is a presentation guide, not evidence that a deployed environment or provider integration has been tested. Available actions depend on the signed-in role, record status, and configured services.

## Before presenting

- Open the Buyer app, a Vendor Owner session, and an authorized Admin session. Keep a Fulfillment Staff session available if demonstrating delivery.
- Use fictional demonstration accounts, sample documents, and Xendit TEST transactions. Keep credentials, verification codes, and environment files off the shared screen.
- Select a location with eligible listings inside the chosen radius. Prepare two eligible stores if showing a multi-Vendor arrangement.
- Prepare one product, one project with a Work Package, one conversation with quotation history, and sample orders at different milestones. Existing samples let you explain later stages without waiting through every deadline.
- Have a sample support request, compliance submission, dispute, invoice copy, and commission statement available for the relevant page tours.
- Rehearse the actual available controls. Use the displayed totals, methods, rate versions, and exact deadlines; the sample quantities and budgets in this guide are illustrative.

## Opening

**Show:** Buyer sign-in or Map, with the Vendor and Admin portals ready in separate windows.

**Say:**

“Good day, everyone. Today, we will demonstrate MateryalPH, a construction-material procurement platform for the Philippines.

the construction sector continues to lag behind, especially in material procurement, which remains fragmented and heavily reliant on traditional practices such as phone inquiries, personal supplier networks, and physical store visits (Dimaculangan, 2023; Cueto, 2022). This continued reliance on traditional and fragmented processes is influenced by the lack of a structured and digital approach to material sourcing. While platforms like Shopee and Lazada offer general retail options for SME contractors and suppliers, they are not optimized for construction materials.
This means many local suppliers have limited opportunities to showcase their products, making it difficult for contractors to efficiently identify, compare, and source materials locally. These particularly affect small and medium enterprise contractors and independent builders, who often rely on manual procurement methods due to limited resources and supplier networks

The time-sensitive nature of construction projects means that delays in sourcing materials can disrupt operations and cause financial losses for SMEs working on fixed-price contracts. This led to the development of MateryalPH, a specialized digital procurement solution for the construction sector, which aims to improve supplier visibility, accessibility, and efficiency in construction material procurement in the Philippine.


“MateryalPH has three applications. Buyers use the mobile app to find suppliers, compare materials, plan project purchases, and track orders. Vendors use the web portal to manage products, inventory, quotations, and fulfillment. Administrators use a separate portal to review verification, resolve cases, and monitor platform activity.

“We will follow a sample renovation project from finding materials to order fulfillment. All payments and financial documents shown in this presentation are demonstrations: DEMO — No real funds or BIR filing.”

## Buyer mobile application

### B1. Sign-in, registration, and location setup

**Show:** Sign-in and registration options. Use a prepared account, then open the location selector.

**Say:**

“A Buyer can register using email or Google and complete the required account information. Email registration includes email verification. Existing users sign in with their registered method, and account recovery is available when needed.

“After signing in, the Buyer selects a location for finding suppliers. They can use their current location, search for an address, select a saved place, or drop a pin. Manual selection remains available when location permission is unavailable.

“The five main tabs are Map, Explore, Projects, Messages, and Profile.”

### B2. Map

**Show:** Map tab → location selector → radius choices → marker → supplier list → filters.

**Say:**

“Map is the starting page for discovering nearby suppliers. The selected location is the center of the search. The Buyer can choose a radius from 5 to 50 kilometers and use filters to narrow the results.

“There are two supplier types. Verified Vendors participate in the MateryalPH marketplace. Directory Suppliers provide attributed Google directory information. Marketplace purchasing and messaging are available through eligible Verified Vendors.

“Tapping a marker opens the supplier preview. The same suppliers are available in a list, so the Buyer can browse even when the map is difficult to use. For a Verified Vendor, we can view the store and available products. A route preview helps explain the journey, with estimated distance and travel time.”

**Presenter note:** Explain VPS as Vendor Performance Score. A Directory label and Google rating describe directory information; a New Vendor label means a marketplace performance score is not yet published. Route previews are estimates, not live delivery tracking.

### B3. Explore

**Show:** Explore → nearby Vendor/product counts → category → search results → sort and filters.

**Say:**

“Explore is the materials catalog. Buyers can browse categories such as cement, steel, roofing, plumbing, and electrical materials, or search for a specific item.

“The page uses the selected location and radius to find eligible marketplace listings. Search results show useful purchasing information such as the product, unit price, store, distance, public availability, and supported fulfillment methods.

“The Buyer can compare results using the available filters and sorting options. Catalog browsing starts with distance, while a text search starts with Best Deal. Best Deal considers several factors, including price and distance, according to the Buyer's ranking preferences.”

### B4. Materials Analytics and Material Price Details

**Show:** Explore → View Materials Analytics → comparable material → variant → graph and current offers.

**Say:**

“Materials Analytics helps the Buyer understand listed prices in the selected area. It groups comparable materials by their specifications and units, then shows recorded daily averages and price changes when enough data is available.

“Opening a material shows its price history and current eligible offers. The Buyer can inspect a specific variant and open a Vendor's product from the offer list.

“These are public listed-price observations for planning. The actual purchase price, quantity, stock, and delivery charge are checked again during the ordering process. Limited data and missing observations are identified on screen.”

### B5. Product Details and Vendor Store

**Show:** Product Details → variant, unit, and quantity → fulfillment options → Vendor Store → Products and Store Profile tabs.

**Say:**

“Product Details explains what the Buyer is purchasing. It shows the product's photos, specifications, unit, variants, price, public availability, and applicable compliance information.

“The Buyer selects the required option and quantity, checks pickup or delivery, and can add the item to the cart or message the Vendor for clarification.

“The Vendor Store provides another way to evaluate the supplier. Products shows eligible listings, while Store Profile explains the business, operating hours, fulfillment options, ratings, and earned badges. The Buyer can save the store as a Favorite Supplier for future purchases.”

**Presenter note:** A PS/ICC indicator describes the platform's evidence status. Internal stock quantities and private verification documents are protected.

### B6. Cart and checkout

**Show:** Cart → Vendor groups → quantity → checkout review. Stop at review unless submitting a prepared demo order.

**Say:**

“The cart organizes items by Vendor. A Buyer may purchase from several stores in one checkout, but each Vendor receives a separate order to manage.

“Before submission, the Buyer reviews quantities, location, fulfillment preferences, and the available cost breakdown. Adding an item to the cart does not reserve it. Inventory and commercial terms are checked during confirmation and acceptance.

“The Vendor confirms the request or sends permitted revisions for the Buyer to review. Payment follows the accepted terms. Online Payment uses Xendit TEST. Cash on Delivery applies to supported delivery orders, and In-Store Payment applies to supported pickup orders.”

**Presenter note:** Online payment success must come from a verified provider event or authoritative reconciliation. Explain the displayed processing fee separately from Vendor commission. Payment screens determine the exact stage at which a method is chosen for each flow.

### B7. Projects: list, overview, and sites

**Show:** Projects → Create Project or prepared project → Overview → Sites.

**Say:**

“Projects supports planned construction purchases. Instead of searching for one item at a time, the Buyer creates a project with a name, site, timeline, and overall budget.

“For example, our project is a home renovation. The Overview shows its planning information and budget summary. Sites stores the locations used by its Work Packages, so supplier comparison can use the correct construction site.

“The Buyer can then divide procurement into manageable Work Packages, such as foundation materials, roofing, or plumbing.”

### B8. Projects: Work Package authoring

**Show:** Work Packages → New Work Package or prepared draft → material lines → budget → Activate and compare.

**Say:**

“A Work Package contains the materials needed for a particular scope. Each line records the material, required unit, quantity, and relevant specifications. The Buyer also provides a package budget and fulfillment details.

“The optional materials estimate helps with planning while drafting. It covers comparable materials and identifies lines that cannot be priced automatically.

“Saving keeps the draft. Activation is a separate action that starts comparison. Work Package allocations must fit within the Project budget, helping the Buyer avoid allocating the same planning funds twice.”

### B9. Projects: comparison and procurement arrangement

**Show:** Find vendors → map/list → comparison option → specification choices → arrangement review → submission checklist.

**Say:**

“The system compares eligible suppliers against the Work Package's requirements, budget, location, and performance preferences.

“A complete option may use one Vendor or an arrangement of several Vendors, with each store supplying assigned materials. Where alternatives exist, the system can present up to three distinct supplier groups alongside complete single-Vendor options.

“We can review coverage, specifications, each supplier's fulfillment method, and the combined materials and delivery estimate. Best Match explains the ranking of eligible arrangements; it is a recommendation for review.

“Choosing an option saves a draft. After reviewing the checklist and checkout, explicit submission sends separate requests to its suppliers. Each supplier keeps its own confirmation, order, payment, and fulfillment.”

**Presenter note:** Displayed system estimates are distinct from published Vendor quotations. Specifications requiring a choice must be resolved. Ready-mixed concrete uses the supported mixer-delivery arrangement. A failing supplier allocation can be replaced while other member orders remain intact.

### B10. Projects: budget and progress

**Show:** Return to Project Overview and Work Package progress.

**Say:**

“The budget view separates allocated funds, pending spend, actual spend, and paid amounts still awaiting refund. This gives the Buyer a clearer view of committed funds and the remaining balance.

“At 90 percent utilization, the system gives an early warning. If a new order would exceed the budget, checkout requires an explicit override with a written reason.

“Work Package progress follows its requirements and related orders. Completion depends on fulfilled requirements or recorded, explicit waivers, rather than merely selecting a supplier.”

### B11. Messages and quotations

**Show:** Messages → store conversation → product reference → Project inquiries → quotation history → latest quotation review.

**Say:**

“Messages brings sales inquiries together by Vendor Store. The Buyer can discuss a product in the main conversation and open related Work Package inquiries through Project inquiries.

“Messages identify the actual public sender and role. Product references and original Work Package requirements give both sides a clear context.

“When an agreement is ready, the Vendor publishes a formal quotation. The Buyer reviews the items, changes, total, fulfillment terms, and deadline. Before accepting the latest quotation, the Buyer explicitly chooses an available payment method.

“Quotation history preserves earlier versions. An ordinary chat message can clarify terms, while formal acceptance records the commercial agreement.”

### B12. Orders, payment, and fulfillment

**Show:** Profile → Orders → Item/Project tabs → Confirmation, Processing, To ship, To receive, and history → Order Details.

**Say:**

“Orders shows what needs the Buyer's attention and what is already progressing. The Item and Project tabs separate the two procurement types.

“Order Details provides the accepted materials and amounts, payment information, expected fulfillment date, and a timeline. For delivery, the Buyer can see authorized assignment information, milestones, and submitted proof. Pickup orders show their own readiness and handover information.

“Fulfillment Messages opens an order-specific conversation for coordination. After handover, the Buyer can confirm receipt or report a problem through the available actions. Payment, fulfillment, refunds, and disputes each retain their own status.”

### B13. Cancellations, refunds, disputes, reviews, and invoices

**Show:** Relevant Profile shortcuts and a prepared Order Detail record. Demonstrate views without submitting a new case.

**Say:**

“Cancellations & Refunds shows the outcome of eligible cancellation requests and the progress of linked refunds. The available action depends on the order's current stage.

“Disputes allows an eligible concern to be recorded with a requested remedy and supporting evidence. The Buyer can follow responses, deadlines, decisions, and any linked refund.

“After completion, eligible Buyers can rate the store and purchased products through My Reviews. An issued Vendor invoice can be viewed from the order, and the Buyer can request a copy or correction when needed.”

**Presenter note:** Buyer cancellation becomes unavailable at Ready for Pickup or Out for Delivery; issue reporting and applicable remedies remain. The normal post-completion dispute window is seven calendar days, subject to non-waivable rights. The rating window is 14 calendar days. A Purchase Order or payment confirmation is distinct from a Vendor tax invoice.

### B14. Profile, preferences, notifications, and Help Center

**Show:** Profile → Account Setting → Saved Locations → Ranking Preferences → Notifications → Help Center → My Support Requests.

**Say:**

“Profile collects the Buyer's account and purchasing tools. It provides shortcuts to Orders, Cart, Cancellations & Refunds, Disputes, and My Reviews.

“The Buyer can manage account details, saved locations, Favorite Suppliers, work information, and notification settings. Account Setting also provides password, authenticator two-factor authentication, device sessions, and account-deletion controls.

“Ranking Preferences keeps Item-Based and Project-Based preferences separate. Each slider expresses the importance of that factor independently.

“Notifications opens relevant updates and their records. Help Center provides Contact Us and My Support Requests, where the Buyer can submit a concern, receive a ticket number, and follow the Admin's responses.”

**Transition:** “We have seen how the Buyer finds materials and submits requests. Next, we will open the Vendor portal to show how those requests are handled.”

## Scope and source notes for the presenter

- Current business rules and later approved amendments take precedence over earlier wording. In particular, use grouped Work Package arrangements, explicit Buyer payment choice for message quotations, the unified store inquiry inbox, and independent ranking sliders as described above.
- Tour the actual implemented navigation. The presence of a generic preview route does not make every preview module an implemented feature. Catalog import is not a current primary action in My Products; the regular product editor is the demonstration path used here.
- Account deletion, sanctions, publishing rate changes, and case decisions can be explained from their review forms or prepared history. Submit them only as intentionally planned demo actions on the relevant fictional records.
- During provider or connectivity problems, explain the displayed state and retry behavior. A screenshot or source-code inspection alone does not establish a successful live operation.
